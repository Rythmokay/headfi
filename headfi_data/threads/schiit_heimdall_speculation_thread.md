### Post #2 by **Member** ()

Martin posted a “mini chapter” on this on the morning of Oct 24:

“And yep, that’s one of the main reasons why Jason and I decided to do this as an actual product after years of saying: No, not unless we find a way to make it worth everybody’s while.

Last year, I spent a few months with a lot of trial and error, and a lot of in-depth studying the different technologies involved, to figure out what parts of a streaming setup play which role, and why, in the sound you get out of it all at the end.

And it turns out that it’s not that hard to get it to sound just right, you just need to first understand and then tightly control the chain of custody of the audio data from the file all the way to the DAC, and after.

And once I got that to work with my first prototype, and Jason had a listen, it was clear that this is now finally worth doing as a product.

It’s relatively easy to sell a box that lets you play back digital audio to a connected DAC, but it’s an entirely different story to offer you a box that outputs digital audio that is at least on par with how Urd performed but that you can just set and forget. Unbox the thing, plug it in, spend less than 5 minutes with setting up Forkbeard and your audio library, spend exactly zero seconds with tweaking audio parameters and settings, and have something that sounds fantastic, always.

Yes, it’s not a device for tweakers. If someone wants to have something to fiddle around with, there are already enough single-board computing based options out there. But if you want a maximum of sound quality with an absolute minimum of fidgeting and learning curve associated with it, our thing will pretty much be the only way for you to get just that.

That’s worth doing to us.

Oh, and because we have full control over the entire stack (“stack” in this context is just fancy way of saying: every piece and layer of firmware and software that runs on it), from the operating system at the bottom of that stack all the way up to the DSP and audio pipeline, we can say with confidence that we’ll be able to offer support (and especially security-related updates) for a decade after we launched that thing. Because we sure as heck don’t want to be the ones responsible for thousands of our customers having a barn door sized security liability in their home networks just because we can’t send out updates and system fixes anymore because we based it on some Android (or other type of OS) version that stops receiving any maintenance or safety-critical updates two years in because the folks who maintain these OSes moved on to the newer, shinier releases. Not with us. What runs on the box is ours, from top to bottom, and will be supported and kept up with for at least a decade. We’re in this for the long haul, because what we’re doing with this will not just be that one product; it is expanding on the work we’ve already started with Forkbeard and will over the coming years form the basis that our entire digital product lineup is built on. It’s the long-term continuation and expansion of what we started with Forkbeard. That’s how and why we can promise that kind of long-term commitment to it all.

What people will be able to see and listen to at CanJam Dallas in a few weeks is still a work in progress. The least finished part of it is the user interface. What we will be using at the show will be a modified version of what we’ve been using during development of the whole system, almost all of it is not what will ship next year. The rest of the software running on the box is about 75% done right now, but it’ll be a close approximation of what you will be able to expect from the box at launch. But, most importantly, the audio pipeline and DSP core is 98% done; what you’re going to hear coming out of it at the Dallas show is what it’ll sound like at launch.

So, yeah. Not a spoof.”

Heimdall, also known as the Whitest of Gods and Gullintanni (Gold-Toothed), is the vigilant guardian of the Bifröst bridge connecting Asgard to Midgard.  He is a son of Odin and nine mothers (often interpreted as the nine waves of the sea giant Ægir), residing at Himinbjörg (Sky Cliffs).  His defining traits include extraordinary senses, allowing him to see hundreds of miles and hear grass growing, and a need for less sleep than a bird.  He possesses the Gjallarhorn, which he blows to warn the gods of approaching enemies, and wields the sword Hofund. He is also credited with fathering the three social classes of humanity under the name Ríg in the poem Rígsþula.
