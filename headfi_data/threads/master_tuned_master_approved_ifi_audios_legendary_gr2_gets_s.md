Master Tuned version of the iDSD GR2 reimagined through the ears of Grammy and Brit Award-winning producer Steve Levine
Southport, UK, October 1, 2026–– 
iFi Audio
, a British pioneer in audio innovation, has announced the launch of the iDSD GR2 MT, a Master Tuned version of its acclaimed iDSD GR2 portable DAC/amp. Reimagined through the ears of Grammy and Brit Award-winning producer Steve Levine, the GR2 MT pairs component-level upgrades with two exclusive, Levine-voiced digital filters to deliver the balance and emotional potency that defines his signature sound. Every sonic decision was guided by Levine himself; it is the sound of a lifetime of studio experience and critical listening.

Steve Levine is a celebrated British record producer whose credits span genres and generations, from Culture Club and the Beach Boys to Gary Moore, Ziggy Marley, and Lemmy and Motorhead. His contribution to music has earned him a number of prestigious industry honours, including a Brit Award, a Musicweek Award, and a Grammy for his work with Deniece Williams. Today, Levine continues to produce exciting new talent from his studio in the heart of Liverpool's Baltic Triangle, including artists signed to his own label, Baltic Jazz Records.

“The goal of any producer is to document the authenticity and emotion behind the artist’s performance,” says Levine. “I have voiced the iDSD GR2 MT with this same goal in mind, and I hope you feel that when you listen”.
Key Upgrades

The GR2 MT builds on the standard GR2 with a series of component-level upgrades. Its high-resolution touch colour display now carries an illuminated silkscreen logo, while the standard FR-4 circuit board is replaced with an aerospace-grade hydrocarbon ceramic PCB offering near-zero signal loss.

The GR2’s OVA2637 with standard surface-mount resistors DAC I/V stage is replaced with OV4627A with audiophile-grade tantalum resistors, and the amp stage moves from standard electrolytic capacitors to Audio Note Kaisei capacitors.

As with its predecessor, at the core of the iDSD GR2 MT is the Burr-Brown PCM1795 DAC, fitted with Levine’s two exclusive filters, alongside JVCKENWOOD’s K2HD Technology, which recovers the depth and nuance of master recordings. The device delivers over 1,500mW RMS power output at 32 ohms, driven through an TFT-LCD display with capacitive touchscreen control.

Wireless listeners are served by Bluetooth 5.4 with aptX Lossless and LDAC, enabling CD-quality playback with no data loss. Hybrid power modes are designed to maximise battery lifespan, connectivity spans both digital and analogue options, and the GR2 MT is fully compatible with the iFi Nexis app for over-the-air updates and advanced control.

### Post #8 by **Member** ()

I’ve been using the original GR2 for a couple of months now, and I’ve been extremely happy with it - 
besides the Battery Health Mode issue for which I currently have an open ticket
. Sound‑wise and feature‑wise, it’s been a fantastic device: great power, stable Bluetooth, excellent touchscreen UI, K2/K2HD, and a warm, cinematic tuning that works beautifully with my IEMs. But after reading through the specs of the new GR2 MT, I have to be honest: I’m annoyed at how iFi handled this release.

The GR2 MT is being marketed as a “Master Tuned” studio‑approved version, but when you actually compare the specs side‑by‑side, it becomes very clear what this really is: 
a GR2 with a refined analogue stage, two extra filters, and a cosmetic upgrade - not a new generation.

Here’s the breakdown:
Both GR2 and GR2 MT have:
Burr‑Brown PCM1795 DAC
OLED colour capacitive touchscreen
Fully balanced analogue topology
Hybrid power mode
K2 + K2HD
Standard iFi digital filters
Bluetooth 5.4 with aptX Lossless, LDAC, LHDC, AAC, SBC
4.4mm + 3.5mm in/out
Same battery, same form factor, same power output
So, the core architecture is identical.
What the MT adds:
Upgraded analogue stage (Class A op‑amps, higher‑grade components, Audio Note Keisei capacitors)
Lower distortion and slightly cleaner midrange
Better separation and layering
Two exclusive Steve Levine filters (one PCM, one DSD)
Illuminated logo
A more “studio‑neutral” voicing compared to the warmer, more cinematic GR2
That’s it. No new DAC. No new Bluetooth chipset. No new power stage. No new screen. No new architecture.

And the price difference? 
GR2: £529
  /  
GR2 MT: £699

A £170 jump for what is essentially a “premium‑component edition” of the same device.

I’m not saying the MT is bad - it’s clearly a refinement, and I’m sure it sounds great. But the way this was released feels off. The GR2 came out, people bought it, settled in, and then just a couple of months later iFi drops a “Master Tuned” version with studio branding and a higher price tag. It’s hard not to feel like this was planned from the start and intentionally held back.

If you already own the GR2, here’s the honest truth: 
You still have 90–95% of the MT.
  The MT is not a GR3. It’s not a replacement. It’s not a new generation. It’s a refinement.

And depending on your listening preferences, the original GR2 might actually be the better match. The GR2 has a warmer, more musical, more cinematic presentation. The MT leans cleaner and more studio‑neutral. If you prefer warmth and body, the original GR2 is still the right choice.

But yes - the timing and positioning of the MT absolutely makes 
early GR2 buyers feel blindsided
. I’m one of them.

I’m happy with my GR2, but I’m not happy with how iFi handled this release.
