Introducing HIFIMAN ISVARNA
ISVARNA’s Semi-enclosed Design with Miniature Dynamic Subwoofer Driver Boosts Low Frequency Response with No Distortion
​

ISVARNA includes a miniature dynamic subwoofer driver that boosts the low frequency response of a planar headphone, while maintaining clear and detailed mid and upper-range output thanks to a crossover point that is set to 200-300Hz.

As the industry’s first hybrid headphone, ISVARNA offers a seamless integration of planar and dynamic drivers. Through its custom-designed crossover circuit, ISVARNA precisely couples the respective frequency response of the two distinct drivers.

“Many music lovers prefer deep bass when listening to music, without compromising on soundstage and overall depth of the sonic output,” says Dr. Fang Bian, President and CEO, HIFIMAN Electronics. “Some manufacturers try EQ tuning, but it often leads to increased, audible distortion and overall deterioration of the sound quality."

“We avoid challenges surrounding phase differential through the angle difference combined with a special installation method for the primary driver and a dynamic subwoofer. These independently developed dyanamic drivers manage low frequencies while planar drivers are responsible for the mid- and high frequencies.”

ISVARNA features HIFIMAN's patented stealth magnet technology. The streamlined cross sectional shape of the Stealth Magnets optimizes and reduces the air turbulence and diffraction between the magnets, allowing the sound to pass between them almost unaffected, without disruption to the sound quality that is often experienced with traditional magnets.

In addition, ISVARNA incorporates HIFIMAN's core technology - Nanometer Thickness Diaphragm, which is under a millionth of a meter thick and extremely light, giving the headphone extremely fast response with outstanding dynamic range and frequency characteristics, as well as low distortion, high transient response, and comprehensive improvement of dynamics and details.

Combined with the Stealth Magnets technology, the nanometer thickness diaphragm material is essential to the sonic output being clear and non-fatiguing, even after hours of listening.

The ISVARNA headband features an advanced ergonomic design, crafted from selected metal materials and precisely cut using high-precision CNC technology, and hand-polished for unmatched comfort with exceptional durability.
Specifications

Frequency Response: 6Hz - 60kHz

Sensitivity: 93dB

Impedance: 16Ω

Weight: 16 ozs

I did try this at CanJam Dallas and posted about it in my 
impressions post
 but here’s the piece I wrote about them:
Isvarna
 — Hybrid headphones aren’t new, but I’m not sure if I can recall a headphone with a planar and dynamic configuration. Most hybrid headphones use a dynamic driver for most of the range, then an electret or balanced armature for extended range. The Isvarna, however, uses a dynamic driver as a woofer and a planar driver for the midrange and treble. I was skeptical of this, as Hifiman’s forays into the closed headphone market have sounded pretty terrible. 

Surprisingly, the Isvarna sounded like a proof of concept that could be viable after a few generations. But in its current state, I can’t recommend it. The bass timbre was noticeably different from its harmonics which made for a very awkward, disjointed crossover. The midrange and treble were pretty gritty and nasal sounding. What’s most interesting is how it seems to behave. I noticed channel imbalance in the midrange that was left-biased. The rep said he didn’t hear it and they measured it to be fine on DMS’ 4128 that was at 
headphones.com
’s table, but my friends also heard the channel imbalance. Part of the issue was that the amp seemed to be faulty as this was repeatable with other headphones. However, on a different amp and different set, I still heard this. But I wanted to see if the imbalance followed the driver by flipping the headphone around on my head. It didn’t; the midrange remained left-biased. 

So, this isolated the problem enough for me to believe this was a head/hair issue. The reps had shorter hair, while mine is long enough that I could see the seal being broken by it. I think leak tolerance is something that should probably be measured.
