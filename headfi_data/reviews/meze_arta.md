## Reviews & Audio Impressions

### Review #1 by  (Rating: 4.00 star(s))

Introduction

Those who have followed my headphone journey for a while probably know that I hold Meze in particularly high regard. Their headphones have always combined exceptional build quality and comfort with a musical and enjoyable sound signature. The Empyrean and later the Elite demonstrated that flagship headphones do not necessarily have to sacrifice long-term listening enjoyment in pursuit of technical excellence.

The Elite in particular has remained one of my favourite headphones for its combination of warmth, dynamics and outstanding comfort. While it was never quite at the technical level of the absolute top performers, its overall presentation made it an extremely enjoyable headphone.

With the Arta, Meze has taken another step forward.

What surprised me most is not any single technical improvement. The Arta does not attempt to impress primarily through an enormous soundstage, exaggerated detail or overwhelming bass. Instead, it delivers what I consider the most coherent presentation of any headphone I have heard so far.

Every part of the frequency response seems to fit perfectly with the rest. Nothing sticks out, nothing feels artificially highlighted, and the overall presentation is unbelievably smooth without becoming boring or technically compromised.

This might sound like a relatively modest achievement compared to the technical extremes some flagship headphones are capable of. In practice, however, achieving this level of coherence while maintaining excellent resolution, spaciousness and bass performance is remarkably difficult.

The Arta is not the outright champion in every individual category. Nevertheless, it might be the most naturally balanced headphone I have encountered.

Build and Comfort

Meze has established itself as something of a reference when it comes to headphone construction. The Empyrean and Elite already impressed with its materials, finishing and ergonomics.

I honestly did not expect Meze to improve much upon the Empyrea/Elite in these areas, yet the Arta manages to do exactly that.

Build quality is once again exceptional. Everything feels carefully designed and executed to the highest standard.

The small cushions on the new headband improve weight distribution and wearing comfort even further. Together with the redesigned earpads, they make an already outstanding suspension system even more comfortable.

This is especially impressive considering how comfortable the Elite already was. I have long considered Meze headphones among the most comfortable high-end models available, and the Arta raises that bar once more.

The differences are not revolutionary, but they are immediately appreciated when wearing the headphone. Meze has refined an already excellent design rather than changing things simply for the sake of novelty.

For my personal preferences, the Arta now represents the highest standard of build quality and comfort I have encountered in a headphone.
Associated Equipment and Amplification

My primary listening chain consisted of a Roon Nucleus Titan feeding a T+A MP2000R G3 and T+A PA2000R/Feliks Audio Envy 25th Anniversary Edition, supported by In-Akustik signal cabling and power filtering. Music was played through Roon using local files and Tidal.

I generally listen at quiet to moderate volume levels.
Sound

The Arta can best be summarized as an exceptionally smooth and coherent headphone with a warm, midbass-focused foundation, a relaxed midrange and well-integrated although slightly subdued treble.

Its most distinctive quality is the complete absence of any frequency region demanding unnecessary attention. The individual parts of the sound signature blend together so naturally that it becomes difficult to identify any obvious weakness in the overall tuning.

The bass provides warmth and body without interfering with the midrange. The mids are positioned slightly further back, contributing to a spacious presentation, while the treble remains smooth without sacrificing the upper-frequency information necessary for an open and detailed sound.

The overall balance is extremely successful. The Arta manages to sound relaxed without becoming overly soft or lacking in technical performance.

What impressed me most is how effortlessly everything comes together. There are headphones with more bass, higher resolution or larger soundstage. Yet very few manage to combine their individual strengths into such a unified presentation.
Bass

The Arta delivers the best bass tuning I have heard from a Meze headphone so far.

As usual with Meze, the Arta places its emphasis in the midbass region. This gives the presentation a pleasant sense of warmth, body and punch without becoming excessively thick.

The midbass is elevated, yet remarkably well controlled. Most importantly, there is no audible bleed into the midrange. Even with its substantial bass foundation, the Arta maintains excellent clarity and never gives the impression that the lower frequencies are overwhelming the rest of the music. A clear refinement over their original house Sound with the Empyrean.

The subbass is not lacking, although the lowest octaves receive less emphasis than the midbass.

For my personal preferences, this is probably the one aspect of the tuning I would change.

As regular readers will know, I am particularly fond of powerful and extended subbass. Headphones like the Audeze CRBN2 and LCD-5S come exceptionally close to my ideal low-frequency balance, with their emphasis placed primarily in the lowest octaves rather than the midbass.

The Arta on the other hand prioritizes midbass body and warmth, creating a fuller and more relaxed foundation.

Interestingly, despite this midbass emphasis, the Arta does not deliver quite the same sense of midbass impact as the Elite. The Elite sounds more dynamic and immediate
Midrange 

The midrange continues the exceptionally smooth character.

It is positioned slightly further back in the presentation, which helps create a pleasant sense of space and prevents vocals and instruments from becoming overly intimate or dominant.

Compared to the Elite, the Arta creates noticeably more distance between the listener and the performers. The Elite places the music closer and delivers a more immediate and dynamic presentation, whereas the Arta takes a somewhat more relaxed approach.

This difference in perspective contributes significantly to their individual characters.

There is no sense of excessive thickness or congestion. Instead, the individual frequency regions transition into one another very naturally.

I would not describe the Arta's midrange as particularly forward or attention-grabbing. Nevertheless, it possesses enough body and presence to sound convincing.
The treble 
follows a similar philosophy.

The lower treble is not particularly emphasized, contributing greatly to the Arta's relaxed and fatigue-free character. There is no unnecessary sharpness or artificial brilliance intended to exaggerate perceived resolution.

Nevertheless, the Arta does not sound overly dark or closed-in.

The upper treble and air frequencies are well represented, providing a convincing sense of openness. Compared to the Elite, however, the Arta offers slightly less air at the very top.

The Elite therefore retains a somewhat airier upper-frequency presentation, whereas the Arta sounds even more cohesive and smoothly integrated.

I generally prefer smoother and slightly darker treble tunings, so the Arta aligns very well with my taste in this regard.

What makes its treble particularly impressive is that it manages to remain completely unobtrusive while preserving an excellent amount of information.

Nothing feels missing, yet nothing is artificially pushed forward.

This balance is among the Arta's greatest strengths and contributes significantly to its outstanding long-term listening qualities.
Technical Performance and Soundstage

The Arta represents another step forward for Meze in terms of detail retrieval.

In fact, I consider it the most detailed Meze headphone I have heard so far.

Fine information is presented effortlessly and without the need for elevated lower treble to create an artificial impression of resolution. The Arta manages to reveal an impressive amount of detail while retaining its remarkably smooth overall character.

Compared to the Elite, the improvement in technical performance is small but noticeable. The Arta resolves finer information more convincingly and generally offers a cleaner, more refined presentation.

Nevertheless, it does not quite reach the absolute highest level of resolution available today.

Headphones like the Audeze LCD-5S and T+A Solitaire P still hold an advantage in ultimate detail retrieval. These headphones are among the strongest technical performers I have encountered, and the Arta falls behind them in this particular area.
The soundstage 
is also very convincing.

It is spacious and noticeably larger than that of the other Meze Models, although it does not reach the enormous dimensions of the most expansive designs available, like Raal 1995 Immanis or Sennheiser HD800S.

The slightly recessed midrange contributes to this sense of space, creating a presentation that feels open without becoming artificially distant.

I particularly appreciate that the stage dimensions feel proportionate to the overall tuning. The Arta does not attempt to create an exaggerated impression of size. Instead, it provides sufficient space for the music to unfold naturally.
Coherence

While the Arta performs at a very high level across practically all technical categories, its greatest achievement deserves particular attention.

I have heard headphones with superior ultimate resolution, stronger subbass and larger soundstages. However, I struggle to think of another headphone where every individual characteristic integrates so effortlessly into the complete presentation. The Bandoss Avija comes to mind, but the Arta bests it in this particular discpline.

The Arta is unbelievably coherent.

The elevated midbass never intrudes into the midrange. The slightly recessed mids create space without making the music feel disconnected. The relaxed lower treble prevents unnecessary aggression, while the upper frequencies retain enough air to avoid an overly dark presentation.

Each of these decisions works in favour of the others.

Nothing feels out of place.

This is something that is difficult to express through individual technical categories. As Meze stated themselves, it has to be experienced to fully understand.
Comparisons

Meze Elite

The Elite is probably the most obvious comparison, considering its position as Meze's previous flagship and the similarities in overall design philosophy.

Both headphones share the characteristic Meze house Sound.

The Elite creates a more intimate and dynamic listening experience. Instruments and vocals are positioned closer to the listener, and its bass impacts have a greater sense of immediacy.

The Arta, on the other hand, presents the music from a more distant perspective. Its soundstage feels more spacious, and the overall presentation is smoother and more relaxed.

In terms of technical performance, the Arta takes the lead even if only slightly.

Nevertheless, I would not consider the Arta a straightforward replacement for the Elite.

Their individual characters are different enough that they complement each other surprisingly well.
Audeze LCD-R

When talking about smoothness the Audeze LCD-R comes to mind, which makes it another interesting comparison .

The Arta is even smoother and more relaxed, which makes the already very smooth LCD-R sound more energetic and direct.

In the bass, the Arta provides greater midbass emphasis, resulting in a warmer and fuller foundation. The LCD-R, on the other hand, offers stronger subbass presence, which aligns more closely with my personal preferences.

Both approaches have their advantages. The Arta delivers greater warmth and midbass body, while the LCD-R provides more emphasis in the lowest frequencies.

The treble balance is also notably different.

The Arta has less lower treble energy, contributing to its smoother and more forgiving character. Interestingly, the situation reverses in the upper treble, where the Arta offers greater presence than the LCD-R.

In terms of technical performance, I would give the LCD-R an advantage in detail retrieval.

It presents fine information more distinctly and generally creates a more energetic listening experience.

The Arta compensates with a slightly larger soundstage
Conclusion

The Meze Arta is an exceptionally accomplished headphone and, in several important respects, the most impressive Meze model I have heard so far.

Build quality and comfort have improved even further compared to the Elite, which was already operating at an extremely high level. The redesigned headband cushions and earpads demonstrate that Meze continues to refine areas where it was already leading the market.

Sound quality has also progressed considerably.

The Arta offers the best tuning and detail retrieval of any Meze headphone so far. Its soundstage is spacious without becoming exaggerated, while the treble achieves an excellent balance between smoothness and openness.

The one aspect I would personally change is the relationship between midbass and subbass. I generally prefer a stronger emphasis in the lowest octaves, and the Arta does not quite satisfy that preference in the same way as my favourite Audeze models.

So is it worth 6000€?

Personally I consider all the high end models in todays market overpriced. Be it Immanis, Susvara, 1266TC, CRBN2 or now the Arta. All seem over the top in that regard with exponential increases over the past decade.

However for the Immanis I "justified" the price tag with being the absolute best in certain technical categories. If you're the absolute best at something, that may cost a certain premium.

And the Meze Arta is in my (not so humble) opinion the most coherent headphone on the Market and therefore something truly special
