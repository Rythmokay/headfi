## Reviews & Audio Impressions

### Review #1 by  (Rating: 5.00 star(s))

Recently I was sent the new Schiit Jot 3 and Lyr 5 headphone amplifiers. I was very interested in listening and using both amps to see which one would fit my use case and how the latest iterations of the Jotenheim and Lyr headphone amps from Schiit have advanced.

My Jot 3 review can be found here: 
https://www.head-fi.org/showcase/schiit-jotunheim-3.29356/reviews#review-44869

Schiit's Lyr 5 is the latest version of their famous Lyr hybrid tube amplifiers. Lyr 5 is very different from the OG Lyr many years ago!

It’s a hybrid tube head amp that goes seamlessly from tube to solid state with the press of a button. Schiit calls this ability to switch from tube mode to SS mode as “Fusion Architecture”. You can have a tube sound amp or if you want you can switch to what’s called a non-tube mode referred to as Mosfet gain. The tube comes with a 6SN7 tube type, a 6H8C / OTK in my unit. A short stubby tube, not like the coke bottle RCA 6SN7’s.

If you’re in the Mosfet gain mode it's kind of like having a solid-state tube but it will last forever. When in solid state mode the tube is completely powered down. This means both the HV voltage rail and heaters! So, your valued NOS tubes will not be used when listening to the solid-state side of Lyr 5. Its like getting two head amps in one.

Another neat feature is it has Forkbeard included in the purchase price. Lyr 5 is biased to run in Class A and seamlessly enter Class AB when needed, dependent on the load being driven…that’s your headphone! The FB app will tell you instantly If you're running in Class A or AB, classy eh! In real time. Smile. Its Pat Pending as well!

FB also give you reporting of system health, over temp and over current alerts. FB gives you complete control of Lyr 5 and even gives you new features like visual volume when using a FB enabled dac. Neat. This means full remote control of input, output, volume, gain and mode! And yes if you don’t want to use FB you don’t have to. Choice.

Live Showing Running in Class A.

Lyr 5 has gobs of power. Power is the same whether you’re using the tube or solid state mode. Power is pretty hefty for sure. 9 watts at 16 ohms, 6 at 32. Honestly there is no headphone that I know of that can not be driven with this power. Nope. Even He6’s and Tungstens. Also Lyr 5 has the same power on both headphone jacks.

It has an internal (no external power brick) and its 100% linear. It has not one but two internal power transformers with a 72 VA rating and 55,000 uf of filter capacitance. Complete with two +/- 100V supplies, two high current +/- 26V supplies and four regulated oversight supplies -2V. +/- 15V and 5V. Not a simple implementation eh.

Gain is Low at 1.2 (1.35 dB) and high at 6.5 (16 dB). Gain can be tube or solid state gain! White led light or Orange led light. White is for SS. Orange is for Tube.

Even though it comes with FB adapter in the box, you still get that IR remote. The price is now $799. So, we are getting up there in price and if you add a decent dac it can be the next step up over the entry level setups Schiit offers. You're getting the latest iteration of FB, a tube and a 64 step ladder motorized volume pot. These things do add cost.

The noise level is pretty much inaudible with all headphones and most IEMs in low gain. YMMV. Running the amp I never saw it leaving Class A. That tells you something!

So some have asked is Lyr 5 just a Lyr + with FB?

Answer: No.

It's really a completely different amp. The power transformer is a new higher capacity transformer, one for HV and one for the heaters. There is a new “tube sleep” mode function. When not using the tube its totally off. Saves your tube and tube longevity. That's this new Fusion Architecture stuff.

The “UI” or how you interact with the new Lyr 5 is new. White and Orange leds that reflect the mode your in and the gain being used. Easy push-button selecting. Leds blink when going from one mode to another and gain. Fun to watch.

There is now a 4.4mm TRRRS jack with HALO Schiits “Mixed Mode Motion feedback” scheme. Its not balanced but allows for connection over a wider set of cables. The impedance using this is 2 ohms.

Continuity A output stage is part of the design which means Lyr 5 can go in and out of Class A without worrying about the crossover issues using NPN and PNP transistors. The NPN and PNP device mismatch is addressed here by this design. Its significantly different than the earlier design of Continuity. 6 NPN and PNP transistors with 4 conducting all the time. With so much power on hand and never really coming out of Class A hardly this is just well, over engineering…but good over engineering.

Coherence topology has been improved in that higher quality MELF resistors and film, silicon NPO caps as well as an improved operating servo. Coherence allows for the elimination of input, output, or interstage coupling caps so your hearing the tube, not the caps! Direct coupled.

Lyr 5 has a fancy motorized 64 step pot that makes soft clicks when moving it. Precise. Adds cost. Allows for remote operation via FB app or the included remote. 1db steps. You might notice that with this type hardware you will have your volume knob in the middle of the knobs travel. It may take you by surprise because with other amps, and their traditional stereo taper pots, you don't usually have to turn the pot very high....so crank it up, the power is there and its not going to hurt anything!

Lyr 5 has a totally new case, upper and lower. Looks really neat.

So having Fusion Architecture and switching between the to there is that $64,000 question: What sounds better?

Answer from Schiit: You tell us!

So is the Lyr 5 balanced. No. There are no XLR inputs, but two RCA inputs, selectable from a simple front panel puh button. Also are RCA preamp outs in the rear if you want to use the preamp function. Nice.

So its not balanced. In the past balanced was desired by many because of the extra power. Well with Lyr 5 your getting maximum power with both the 4.4 mm jack and Se jack. It really doesn’t matter if your using balanced of single ended. Done. And the 4.4mm jack is wired to support Schiits unique Halo mixed mode motion feedback system. Choices.

Sonic Experience and Comments:

Wow, so much to say here. Lyr 5 is a really different beast to me. For many it will be the holy grail of hybrid tube amps. For others maybe not so. Let me explain.

The lyr 5 comes across a euphonic sounding to me and with different cans it’s a different “euphonic” experience. After many years of tube brain, SS to me is my preferred type of amplifier. I like the crisp, accurate and articulate sound vs the warm soupy tubey sound. Its not that strange here that I actually prefer the SS side of Lyr 5, Yeah sorry blasphemy but we all hear differently. I find the Lyr 5 so musically clean, clear and just buttery tubey at times. Effortless sound.

DCA Expanse, pairs nicely.

Even though Forkbeard isn’t required for my headphone setup, its great in the 2 ch room with my Kara, G2 and Wotan. In the desktop headphone room, I am within easy arms reach of my stuff. I want to turn the vol pot, adjust with switches etc. Honestly with all the recent hype about displays, phone displays etc…I just want to listen to music with headphones and not have to look at my phone screen via an app to make stuff happen on my headphone amps. I have several exotic brand X AIO’s with phone apps as well. I appreciate the technology here. But in this use case I don’t use their apps either. Old school. Sorry. Well, not. Smile.

Operation is a bit different having push buttons vs traditional up/down switches, Switching gains in either tube or SS mode requires a press of a push button an 8 second of delay and a blinking led while the gain changes. Mentioned before the cool while led color indicates your in SS mode. To change over to tube mode leaving those Mosfets behind, you long press the gain switch button for more than 2 seconds and the changeover starts. It takes 24 seconds to go into Tube mode from the SS side of things. The led blinks in orange until the tube is warmed up. Neat design here. And yes the power switch is still on the back!

I have found listening in the SS mode setting the volume for a comfortable sound in low gain and then pressing a long press on the front panel button to go into tube mode and the gain is the same just now orange in color with the led. I cycled thru from Tube Low gain to SS low gain, SS high gain to Tube high gain etc several times and watched the blinking led light show. Neat. Takes time yes, 8 seconds and 24 seconds as mentioned. Not as fast a flicking a switch for gains, but we are experiencing Fusion architecture in action. Its not a good idea to switch back and forth in quick succession for tubes in general. But for extended use over time turning off the B+ and heaters is a good thing. But even a good thing can be abused. So beware.

Also if the unit is in a switching mode, power down the unit is not a good idea, I have found. You might power up and get caught in a two blinking white leds forever. Had to power down for more than a few seconds to get re-powered up with the blinking orange leds and back to normal. Most folks would not even do this. But I test.

I have several sets of headphones here…from very sensitive easy to drive cans to some really harder to drive cans. With the amount of power packed in Lyr 5, there is no issue driving anything that I can think of even He6’s or Tungstens. Yes I have had all these here over the years…they all worked wonderfully with Lyr 5 in either mode.

All of these DCA headphones worked well with Lyr 5

I just recently reviewed the new Schiit Jot 3, which I highly recommend and like personally. It fits my use case very well. That said Lyr 5 is a different beast of an amp. I find my self in love with the tube side in high gain for most of my cans. The SS side is ok, but the SS Jot 3 wins my heart here. The SS side IMO is very similar to Jot 3. All headphones I have from easy to drive, like the Hedd D1’s to the hard to drive Expanse and E3’s. No issues with power. None.

The idea behind Lyr 5 to be able to listen with a tube and a SS amp is really a neat idea and how to switch back and forth sensibly is also a good engineering accomplishment. Also adding in a more sophisticated FB is also nice. Also the FB in Lyr 5 has the ability to tell you if your in Class a or going into class ab, add visual volume when using a FB enabled dac. Honestly this is a question that many of us have asked, are we in Class A or not. But on the other side of the coin with 6 watts into 32 ohms, there are very few times with headphones that you be leaving Class A with Lyr 5. So why? Hmmm. Because they can I guess.

Add all the circuit improvements, from the transformers to a motorized relay ladder volume control, perfect level matching, full dc coupling from input to output, MELF resistors, separate high voltage and low voltage supplies. Lyr is really a step up from the lyrs of old.

Depending on whether you're into tubes in general and want the backup of a very good and powerful solid-state amp, Lyr 5 provides you with all of this and more. If you're not into tubes and don't want to chase NOS tubes and costs, Jot 3 is less cost and has pretty much the same power etc. Much to think about!

Alex
