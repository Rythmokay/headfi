## Reviews & Audio Impressions

### Review #1 by  (Rating: 4.00 star(s))

Hi everyone, I’m back with a review of Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro and Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL Dongle DACs. I received this IEM from Nicole, who kindly provided it for review purposes. Thank you to Nicole and the Kiwi Ears team.

Here are my subjective impressions as an audio enthusiast. To summarize, here are the pros and cons of the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL and Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro:

Allegro BAL (+)
Having 2 balanced ports makes it quite easy for A/B testing, allowing you to compare the sound of one IEM/headphone with another of the same model that might be abnormal or already modified.

If you need to listen to music in an intimate setting with a partner, you no longer have to share a single IEM/earphone, now each individual can enjoy full stereo because both ports are active simultaneously.

Free from dangling cables.

Its 180mW output power is enough to drive most IEMs on the market and some high-sensitivity headphones.

Features a 5-band PEQ.

The shape is still relatively compact and solid, similar to other T-shaped dongle DACs released by Kiwi Ears, but this one is the widest.

Has a sound signature that remains similar to other Kiwi Ears T-model DAC series, which is neutral leaning slightly bright.

Allegro BAL (-)
Even though it has a PEQ, competitors already support more than 5-band PEQ, come with companion apps, and can be accessed across various devices.

If you have a laptop with 2 adjacent USB Type-C ports, there is a high chance your second slot will be blocked by this dongle DAC.

As for the filter selection button, sometimes it has been pressed but the light refuses to change, and the filter options don't sound any different to my over 30 year old ears.

Compared to lower tier versions like the Allegro Mini and Coda, the noise floor is slightly more audible.

If you are a low volume listener, it is quite difficult to set the volume as low as possible because, at its default setting, the lowest volume is still not low enough for me, especially for IEMs. I recommend that if there is a pre-amp option in the application you use, you should give it a try.

Allegro Pro (+)
Features a 5-band PEQ.

The shape is still relatively compact and solid, similar to other T-shaped dongle DACs released by Kiwi Ears.

Features SE (Single-Ended) and balanced jack connectivity, and both ports can be used simultaneously.

Has a neutral sound tuning leaning slightly bright.

Free from dangling cables.

Its 170mW output power for balanced is enough to drive most IEMs on the market and some high-sensitivity headphones.

Allegro Pro (-) Same as the Allegro BAL.

These impressions are based on my preference for a V-shaped bright sound signature. Please don't use these impressions as a reference, as every ear is different and I'm only sharing my personal experience. Always trust your own ears and audition if possible. If you find any mistakes, feel free to comment.

Initially, I wanted to separate this into 2 reviews, but since these 2 dongle DACs are not vastly different in character, I decided to combine them to make it easier for readers to choose, after all, their prices and decoding capabilities are also quite similar.

The specifications for the Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro are as follows:
Connection method: USB Type-C 

Output terminals: 3.5mm single-ended / 4.4mm balanced (dual output)

Output power: 68mW @ 32Ω (3.5mm) / 170mW @ 32Ω (4.4mm)

SNR: > 113dB

THD+N: < 0.006%

Crosstalk: Less than -65dB at 3.5mm output / Less than -100dB at 4.4mm output (at 32Ω)

Dual ESS9603 Sabre amplifier chips

PCM support: 44.1kHz to 384kHz / 32bit

Construction: All-aluminum alloy body

Compatibility: Windows, macOS, Android 5.1+, and compatible iOS

The specifications for the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL are as follows:
Connection method: USB Type-C (direct plug-in)

Output terminals: 4.4mm x 2 balanced (dual output)

Output power: 180mW @ 32Ω (4.4mm)

SNR: > 113dB

THD+N: < 0.005%

Crosstalk: Less than -100dB at 4.4mm output (at 32Ω)

PCM support: 44.1kHz to 384kHz / 32bit

Noise Floor: 5mV

Construction: All-aluminum alloy body

Compatibility: Windows, macOS, Android 5.1+, and compatible iOS


Additionally, on both dongle DACs, there is a button that functions to change the filters as follows:
Blue: Game Mode

Red: Music Mode

Green: Theater Mode

Blank: Default

Honestly, these filters are barely noticeable to my over 30 year old ears, and its operation sometimes misses, after the button is pressed, the light does not change, so I cannot explain it any further.

As for the included accessories, they are very minimal,  you only get the dongle DAC along with the product information paper.

Regarding the price, the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL is currently around $49 and the Kiwi Ears Allegro pro around $59. 

Usage Experience

Both devices share similarities in the materials used,  which is a solid aluminum alloy. So far, the most significant difference, in my opinion, lies only in their appearance and port availability. The Allegro BAL has two 4.4mm ports, while the Allegro Pro has a 3.5mm port and a 4.4mm balanced port with a larger power output.

When I first tried both of these dongles, one annoying thing for me was the volume level, which I felt couldn't go down to the lowest level I desired. Consequently, even though I had set the volume as low as possible on both my PC and Android, the sound was still a bit too loud for me. It is a bit cumbersome if you use high-sensitivity IEMs, you have to use a pre-amp application to lower the volume via software, but this shouldn't be a problem if you are a middle-level volume listener.

As for the power from the balanced port, which can deliver up to 180mW, it is quite capable for various high-sensitivity IEMs and headphones. However, it is very unfortunate that the noise floor is still noticeable. There is a slight background hiss that is somewhat masked when you play songs/music, but during silence, this hiss is quite annoying. Oddly enough, this hiss is quite noticeable when connected to a DAP or Android phone, but fairly minimal when connected to a PC.

Regarding the tonal sound, it is still in the same vein as other Kiwi Ears Allegros I have tried, which is neutral with a slightly bright hint. However, because the Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro and BAL have more capable power, I feel the dynamic impact in some songs is better compared to the Kiwi Ears Allegro Mini and Coda. Meanwhile, regarding technicalities like resolution and detail, I do not feel any significant improvement from the Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro and Allegro BAL, the sound still feels a bit analytical.

The most noticeable advantage of the Allegro Pro and Allegro BAL versions, in my opinion, is their power, which reaches around 180mW, providing ample headroom for most high-sensitivity IEMs and headphones. As for operating temperature, I feel the Allegro Pro gets slightly warmer than the Allegro BAL, but it remains quite comfortable.

An interesting thing about the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL is that, with the availability of two 4.4mm balanced ports, we can do A/B testing by comparing the sound of the same model of IEM or headphone with another that might be abnormal or modified, since the volume output is identical. Or, if you have a partner or friend who wants to listen to music together, you can simply plug another headphone/IEM with similar power requirements into the other port.

Conclusion

So, are the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL and Kiwi Ears Allegro Pro worth buying? As T-shaped dongle DACs, which are somewhat rare on the market, I feel a price of around 50 bucks is still quite affordable if you need a practical, cable free dongle with more than enough output power. If you are someone who likes to share songs to enjoy together with a partner, but wants it to feel more intimate without disturbing the surroundings with a phone speaker that sounds tinny when turned up, you might want to consider getting the Kiwi Ears Allegro BAL. Furthermore, if you are an audiophile who often compares the sound of one IEM/headphone to another, you can also use the Allegro BAL as your A/B testing tool. That's all, fellas. Thank you for reading. Cheers.
