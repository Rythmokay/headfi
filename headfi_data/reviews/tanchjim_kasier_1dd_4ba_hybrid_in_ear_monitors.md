「SOUND-SIGNATURE」

It sounds closer to neutral lines that focuses on clear vocal delivery. The elevated bass and treble have well-balanced presence in the mix. The vocals are on the intimate side with a stronger upper mid-range emphasis. The bass sounds punchy with a natural presence and decay. The treble has adequate brilliance, just not the airiest presentation.
「
BASS
 ／
LOW-END
」
►
The bass prioritize on giving a satisfying punch rather than filling the room with bass presence. It is more upper bass oriented than low bass. The lateral speed is moderately fast as it still allows the bass notes to linger momentarily without muddying your music definition. There is enough warmth to keep a natural playback.
►
The sub-bass were given ample of presence. It sustains long enough to set things mildly atmospheric. The attack pressure isn't that powerful, but you should be able to hear the subtle vibration when the underlying tremor and rumble is dispersed. When the beats-drop, it doesn't hit the lowest. But it hits deep enough to create a believable contrast.
►
The upper bass have good density without being overly thick with its bass notes. The attack seems tighter and sharper in definition. It makes percussive bass hits sounds taut and impactful.
►
Kick-drums sounds big, thick and sturdy. There is a hint of wooliness behind the thud, but it doesn't sound limp with its impact.
►
Drum-strike have decent clarity. It slams hard enough with appropriate sense of fullness and richness.
►
Bass line guitars offer a good level of sustained-tone with acceptable attack amplitude. The rate of humming and droning could be better, but it doesn't sound too monotonous.
►
Ghetto bass isn't the bounciest, while bass-drop elements have a fine rendition.
►
Bass control sounds tidy as there is no bleeding or smearing the upper registry.
" In short, the bass performance is just average at best. It serves as a good accompaniment to covers the low-end spectrum."
「MID-RANGE ／VOCAL」
►
This is clearly a mid-range centric set. Vocal and instruments that lives in this region do standout above the bass and treble elements. Vocal placement sounds wide and close-up but not overly frontal. It gives its lead vocal a clear delineation from the background elements.
►
Speaking of texturing and detailing, the mid-range goes for a smoother approach. You really need to pay closer attention to pick up the surface layers on vocal phrasing. It is not void of detail, but it is more subtly presented. Which makes hearing them sounds pleasant and soothing.
►
Fundamentally, the mid-range is well founded. It handles hollowness and thinning effect well. Of course, as the notes progress higher in the range, the sound gets denser and more solidified. Overall, it just has enough warmth and brightness to keep its mid-range sounding natural with minimal colouration.
►
MALE/LOW-PITCHED
 :
 Male vocal performance may not be its strongest suit. Bass and baritone ranges though, sound forward will have a leaner sense of body. Seemingly, the resonating low, deep chesty voice lacking abit of sustained tone to bloom completely. As a result, male vocalist that lives at the lower part of region will sound more submissive than immersive. You can compensate that with warmer pairings to bring the best out of it. Thankfully, tenor delivery sounds more alive as it soars higher towards the apex. However, on certain tracks, male vocalist can sound abit pitchy, especially at louder volume.
►
FEMALE/HIGH-PITCHED
 :
 They performs more favourably. Tonally, they sound sweeter. The vocal weight and density are much more consistent here. Whilst I said that, the lower tone, especially contralto range seems to apply the less engaging playstyle. However, once the vocal enters the mezzo-soprano band, their performance gets livelier and euphonic. There is a good sense enthusiasm behind their voices which I appreciate. The emotional delivery feels more direct. Every syllabus now carries more weight. The airy and breathy complexion to the voice is there but faint. It performs best with ACG/K-pop and J-pop trackcs. They sound vibrant and euphonious. I had a good time listening to them.
►
Regardless of its tonal flaws, the shouted or screamy vocal is handled well for as long as you don’t crank the volume. Whilst it is on the more energetic side, I don't find the upper mid-range energy to be fatiguing. It is free from any sort of harshness except for some minor grain on some poorly recorded tracks.
►
Guitar pluck plays well. It's got good balance of bite and note-weight with a natural overtone.
►
Piano playback sounds direct and uncoloured. Every tap is well distinct with a pleasant tonality.
►
Violin and cello sounds clear and well express throughout the mix.
" Overall, it favours its female vocal performance more so than its male counterpart. Female ACG/J-pop and K-pop music just feels more at home. As for the rest of it, they can perform more than satisfactorily. "
​
「TREBLE ／ HIGHS」
►
The treble is relatively well-balanced in the mix. It is not too bright nor it is too dark. It is not the airy or open sounding type of presentation. Most the brilliance do comes from the lower half of the treble. It provides just about enough brightness to spark the essential high elements.
►
On overage, it responds very fairly smooth. There is no offensive glare or spiciness at normal listening volume. Sibilance control is excellent. It doesn't trim the Ts and Ss consonants completely, but sooth it.
►
Treble definition may not be its forte. But often, the high-end components just come through the way you expected it to be. It doesn't sharpen nor blurred them. It sounds rather natural. Unless you're seeking for a crispy and crunchy high notes, then this is not it.
►
Hi-hats has neutral tone. Even when "chick-ing" constantly, the definition is clear.
►
Cymbals sounds smooth and inoffensive. Cymbal crash doesn't sound splashy while the underlying wash has natural flowy quality.
►
Drum-snare sounds slightly further back in the mix but still sounds well-articulated.
►
E-guitar has a fine blend of warmth and musical engagement. It is not overly energetic but just enough for a lively presence.
►
Glockenspiel focuses more on initial ding than harmonic overtone.
►
Electronic elements in generals will have a more rounded edge to the zing, ring, ding, buzz etc.
►
It is quite a safe treble. Its smooth nature makes its suitable to listen on long listening sessions. Meanwhile, its analytical prowess is just average.
" Overall, it is a smooth, refined highs that is easy to listen. The treble performance is one the better multi-driver set at this price point. "

It has a stereo-like 
STAGING 
with slight out of head experience. It is not what I would a call holographic. Regardless, it’s got good width and headroom with shallower sense of depth.
IMAGING PERFORMANCE
 is just average. Most of the sound projections sounds accurate with run of the mild level of sharpness.
INSTRUMENT SEPARATION
 is solid for this tier; the vocals and instruments have decent delineation. Only on less a masterful track, it can struggle a little.
DETAIL-RETRIEVAL
 is just average at best. The macro and micro elements have good balance of contrast without sounding "Low-Res".
DYNAMIC
 wise, it is broad enough to capture the flow of the music. The loud and soft expressions have decent divergence to keep the musical elements going.
TONE & TIMBRE? 
Overall, it carries a pleasant to tonality. The one thing that may take away some of the naturalness is perhaps its smooth and rounded attack-edge. When it comes to the sustain, decay and overall note-weight it is on point.
DRIVER & SYNERGY?
 The driver quality is up to its task. However, it is not the easiest to drive. I've noticed that I need to crank 15-20% more power than usual. It will benefit from having a greater amplification rate. As for the choices of DAC, a neutral source works just fine if you're aiming to listen to its intended tuning. However, if you want to make it a better all-rounder, pairing with a warmer source will get the job done.
PRICE?
 At 
$129
, the price performance is solid. Plus, the overall packaging and accessories do add up. You really get what you paid for.
✧ ════ •BUILD/ PACKAGING/COMFORT• ════ ✧
​
The faceplate design does look a little "budget”, but it is well made. The shell is around medium-sized. The body width is not too thick with a nozzle that is on larger side. For smaller ears, you might want to use a smaller sized ear-tips to get the best fit and comfort. It is well vented with no driver flex issue.
The stock cable has been improved from previous generation of Tanchjim model. It is well behaved and lightweight. There is no swappable plug but a fixed 4.4MM balanced termination. They've provided us with a white pocketable case with 2 sets of Tanchjim’s latest TP500 eartips (Bass & Treble), sizing from S to L. And that’s about it.
→ CONCLUSION ←
​
The Tanchjim Kasier feels like it is meant for ACG, K-pop/J-pop, particularly female vocalist. The Kasier shines the best with that specific library. It makes them sound engaging yet pleasant to listen. However, if you listen to a wider broad of genres, it is best to pair it with a warmer DAC/AMP. To my experience, it will turn it into a much more versatile gear when it needs to. Still, expectations need to be catered. It is no flagship-killer or a budget flagship at that. But it performs right on the money for the asking price. For around $100~200 mark, I'd recommend checking the Tanchjim Kasier especially, when you're an anime music lover. It certainly didn't disappoint me in that regard.
