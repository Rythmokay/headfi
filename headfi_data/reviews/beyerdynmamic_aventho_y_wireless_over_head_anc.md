## In-Depth Reviews & Sound Impressions

### Review #1 by  (Rating: 4.50 star(s))

Electro Audio World's Take :

The 
Beyerdynamic AVENTHO Y
 is a more exciting model among the new generation of Bluetooth headphones than I had anticipated. Right out of the box, it impresses with its build quality, color options, user friendly controls, and most notably an exceptional battery life, aided by a replaceable battery.

Its compact, foldable design also makes it a compelling choice for long term use. However, for an audiophile, practical features are only part of the story. The real question is whether this more affordable wireless model from Beyerdynamic delivers a listening experience that keeps you coming back for more. Let’s find out if it truly does.

With a price tag of around $150, the AVENTHO Y also features effective ANC. While it offers several advantages rarely seen at this price point, its sonic character in my opinion deserves even more attention than the technical specifications might suggest.
Design, Build, and Comfort

While maintaining its signature circular ear cup aesthetic, Beyerdynamic has given the AVENTHO Y a more contemporary look. The aluminum support arms enhance structural integrity, and the foldable design makes the headphones highly practical for daily commuting and travel. Available in four distinct colors Liquid Black, Blue Fusion, Soft Jade, and Peach Blush the headphones possess a unique character that sets them apart from the typical, generic black plastic models on the market.

I opted for the Blue Fusion version, it is quite stylish and elegant. At 316 grams, it cannot be considered particularly lightweight; however, the padded headband and ear cushions help distribute this weight evenly. Naturally, the level of comfort especially during extended use will vary depending on an individual’s head shape and ear size. The closed back acoustic design provides effective passive isolation even before the ANC feature is engaged. I appreciate this.

Regarding the design, the aspect I value most is the emphasis on repairability. The ear cushions are attached via a magnetic system, and the battery can be replaced by the user through a compartment located beneath the left ear cushion. In a product category where headphones often become completely non functional turning into electronic waste once their battery reaches the end of its lifespan, this is a significant advantage.
Sound Quality

The AVENTHO Y is an interesting subject for discussion, as there is no clear consensus among existing reviews regarding its tonal balance. Some users describe an energetic, bass forward sound profile with an impressively wide soundstage.

Others, however, find the default sound signature somewhat lacking in the low frequency range, and further note that the upper mid frequencies can occasionally become intrusive on certain recordings. Acknowledging these differing perspectives is more important than assuming these headphones will offer an ideal sound for everyone.

My approach is to evaluate them primarily as portable, wireless headphones, paying close attention to vocal balance, bass definition and separation, and how they handle recordings with prominent high frequency content. Let’s see what they have to offer.
Bass

The low frequencies have enough presence to make modern pop, electronic music, and rock enjoyable; however, the AVENTHO Y is certainly not a basshead headphone. Its appeal depends on how much weight you expect from the default tuning, but as an audiophile, I found it quite pleasing.

Bass lines are easier to appreciate when they manifest as clearly defined notes rather than a constant wall of low frequency energy throughout a recording. With the right material, the bass provides a solid foundation without needing to overpower any other instruments. That said, listeners who expect a deep, powerful physical impact right out of the box might want to experiment with an equalizer a feature I found to be quite useful.

This is where the app comes into play. With a modest adjustment to the low frequencies, you can add fullness to kick drums and electronic bass lines without compromising vocal clarity. I would advise against over boosting, as adding too much bass can cause the midrange frequencies to sound thicker than intended.
Midrange

The mid frequencies determine whether the AVENTHO Y is capable of more than just casual background listening. Vocals require sufficient presence to remain intelligible, while instruments like guitars, piano, and strings demand a convincing sense of body and separation.

The AVENTHO Y can lend a direct, forward character to vocals a quality that works well for podcasts and recordings where the singer needs to take center stage. A potential drawback is that energy in the upper midrange can become quite pronounced with certain voices; recordings that are already aggressive in nature may reveal this more readily than softer, acoustic material.

I wouldn’t necessarily describe the AVENTHO Y as "neutral." Its appeal depends more on personal taste and careful fine tuning. The good news is that the app offers a practical way to refine the sound, rather than forcing listeners to settle for a fixed, one size fits all profile. With the right adjustments, I was easily able to achieve the sound I wanted.
Treble

The upper frequencies possess enough energy to preserve the crispness of cymbal strikes and the sharp edges of percussion; however, depending on the recording and the listening volume, this brightness can become quite prominent. You might notice this particularly in sharp vocal recordings or tracks that already emphasize upper midrange detail.

The AVENTHO Y lacks the level of refinement I would expect from a higher end audiophile headphone. That said, provided the recording is well balanced and the EQ is set sensibly, the treble remains quite enjoyable. Rather than making drastic changes to the overall frequency response, I would suggest starting with a subtle reduction in the upper frequencies.
Soundstage and Imaging

For a closedback wireless headphone in this price category, the spatial presentation is an intriguing aspect of the AVENTHO Y experience. Instruments can occupy distinct positions across the stereo image, helping the music feel organized rather than just a flat wall of sound. The soundstage is better than I had anticipated.

It does not replicate the depth or effortless openness of a high quality open back wired headphone nor would I expect it to. Its strength lies in providing effective separation within a portable design. Ultimately, I find the clarity and coherent stereo image particularly useful for everyday listening. I personally found it quite enjoyable.
Listening via Bluetooth with the Apple iPhone 14 Pro Max:

The iPhone 14 Pro Max serves as a highly practical audio source for these headphones, reflecting real world usage conditions. The pairing process is straightforward. The AVENTHO Y supports AAC one of the primary Bluetooth audio codecs used by iPhones along with SBC. Since the device lacks support for aptX or LDAC, Android users seeking these transmission options will need to look elsewhere.

When paired with an iPhone, the AVENTHO Y is best viewed not merely as a traditional wired headphone, but as a complete, portable audio system. It is perfectly suited for music streaming services, podcasts, downloaded music, and video content. The multipoint connection feature is also highly functional and works seamlessly if you frequently switch between your phone and a laptop.

It is worth noting that sound quality is not determined solely by Bluetooth codec support. Many other factors such as driver performance, acoustic tuning, the fit of the headphones, digital signal processing, and the quality of the recording itself have a significant impact on the sound. In my view, AAC provides high quality, more than adequate sound for daily listening; I personally haven’t felt that anything was lacking. Furthermore, keep in mind that if a tonal balance doesn’t align with your personal preferences, no amount of codec support can fix it.

Personally, I prefer to use the beyerdynamic app to create a comfortable sound profile and keep that profile consistent for my everyday listening. This allows the AVENTHO Y to reveal its true strengths, without the need to turn every listening session into an exercise in equalizer adjustment.
Noise Cancellation, Battery Life, and Daily Use

The ANC (Active Noise Cancellation) feature is highly beneficial, especially when walking outdoors or whenever you wish to reduce persistent ambient noise. Whether you want to minimize the sounds of public transport, the intense noise of a busy workplace, or everyday background chatter, you can use this feature freely without worrying about the battery draining too quickly.

In my previous tests with headphones from other brands, I would often turn off ANC relying instead on passive isolation just to preserve battery life; with the AVENTHO Y, however, that is not a concern. Even with ANC enabled, I managed to get nearly 52 hours of playback over a week of listening likely the longest battery life I have ever experienced with ANC equipped headphones. It is worth noting, however, that this should not be interpreted as "total silence"; performance can vary depending on the type of noise and how well the ear cups seal around your ears, but it is certainly effective.

The transparency mode is very convenient for hearing announcements or conversing with others without needing to remove the headphones.

Battery life is one of the device's most remarkable features. According to Beyerdynamic, it offers over 90 hours of playback with ANC turned off, and over 60 hours with ANC enabled. A five minute charge provides up to six hours of playback, while a full charge takes approximately one hour.

These figures are provided by the manufacturer. Keep in mind that the same results may not be achieved at every volume level. In my own tests, I noted that it took about 1 hour and 12 minutes to reach a full charge.

The user replaceable battery and magnetic ear pads are features that could make the AVENTHO Y particularly attractive to those who intend to use their headphones for many years. I would argue that this is a more meaningful advantage than simply adding a feature that would rarely be used; for someone who truly values their headphones, such details become highly significant over the long term.
Comparisons :

1- Sennheiser ACCENTUM Wireless vs. 
Beyerdynamic AVENTHO Y

The Sennheiser ACCENTUM Wireless is a strong alternative for those seeking a balanced, everyday pair of headphones without paying a flagship level price. It features a warm sound signature and supports various Bluetooth codecs including aptX and aptX HD on compatible devices. While it offers an impressive battery life of up to 50 hours and solid ANC performance, it isn't necessarily the best in its class; in this regard, the two models are quite comparable.

The AVENTHO Y holds an advantage by offering a significantly longer battery life, Bluetooth 6.0 support, and a user replaceable battery. Its repairable design and magnetic ear pads are particularly notable benefits. The Sennheiser, on the other hand, provides greater flexibility for Android users in terms of codec support and may appeal more to listeners who prefer the brand’s signature warmer sound profile.

Ultimately, the choice depends on your personal priorities. The AVENTHO Y is highly compelling for those who value longevity and durability, whereas the ACCENTUM stands out as a serious option for those who prefer Sennheiser’s approach to sound and require aptx support.
2- JBL Live 770NC and 
Beyerdynamic AVENTHO Y

The JBL Live 770NC seems designed to offer a fun listening experience. Its default sound profile is more bass forward than that of the AVENTHO Y, making it an attractive choice for electronic music, hip hop, and modern pop. The JBL app allows for useful sound customization. Furthermore, with its noise cancellation feature and long battery life, it serves as an excellent companion for your travels.

The AVENTHO Y, on the other hand, stands out with a different set of advantages. It features a replaceable battery, magnetic ear pads, and a more modern Bluetooth standard. Its sound character can be quite impressive once adjusted; however, if you are sensitive to energy in the upper mid frequencies or are looking for a fuller bass response, you may need to perform some fine tuning.

If you are seeking a stronger bass emphasis and a comprehensive feature set for daily use, the JBL Live 770NC is an option worth considering. The AVENTHO Y, meanwhile, may be a more compelling alternative for long term use especially if the product's longevity focused features are just as important to you as its sound quality.
3- AVENTHO 300 vs. 
AVENTHO Y

The AVENTHO 300 is another model in Beyerdynamic’s wireless lineup, positioned in a higher segment. Utilizing German made STELLAR.45 drivers, this model supports aptX Adaptive and aptX Lossless formats (when used with compatible sources) and also offers Dolby Atmos support with head tracking capabilities. It would perhaps be more accurate to describe this model with its ANC system and high end feature set as one tailored for listeners seeking a more immersive experience when enjoying music, movies, or traveling.

The AVENTHO Y, on the other hand, features a simpler design. It comes equipped with a well tuned 45mm dynamic driver, supports AAC and SBC codecs, and includes a standard ANC system; in other words, it lacks the advanced audio technologies found in the AVENTHO 300. In exchange, it offers a much more accessible price point, impressive battery life, and user replaceable components.

If your priorities are nuanced sound quality, support for advanced codecs, and a spatial audio experience, the AVENTHO 300 is the more ambitious choice though do keep the price difference in mind. However, if you are looking for a practical, long lasting pair of headphones one that can run for days on a single charge and remains usable for years thanks to its repairable design the AVENTHO Y stands out as a highly compelling option.
Final Verdict

The 
Beyerdynamic AVENTHO Y
 is a practical pair of wireless headphones that places an unusual emphasis on long term usability. Features like its exceptional battery life, replaceable battery, and swappable ear pads are highly functional, while Bluetooth 6.0 with multi device support offers great convenience for everyday use.

As for its sound signature, it delivers the energetic and lively presentation one expects from a Beyerdynamic product. While the default settings may work perfectly for some, others might find it beneficial to tweak the EQ either to balance the upper mid frequencies or to add a bit more weight to the low end.

Naturally, this model is not a direct alternative to the more ambitious AVENTHO 300. It stands out as a more affordably priced version. While it may lack the codec flexibility offered by some competing models, the quality remains quite high for those who use an iPhone.

What makes the AVENTHO Y compelling is its combination of affordability, durability, and repairability. After all, why should a product be discarded or effectively become a disposable item simply because its battery has worn out?

For those who value ease of use and the ability to keep a product for many years, the AVENTHO Y is definitely a model worth considering. As for audiophiles who prioritize sound quality and tonal nuances above all else, I recommend trying the headphones out before purchasing and spending some time experimenting with the EQ settings via the app.

While the design incorporates many practical solutions, the sound signature must ultimately align with your personal taste. Personally, I am quite pleased with what this product offers at this price point I hope you will be, too.
